# Tastecraft benchmark: dishes with and without Qloo

Run on 2026-10-20 with `npm run benchmark` (scripts/benchmark.ts), model `claude-opus-5-5`. Scores recomputed on 2026-10-20 with `npm run benchmark -- --rescore` (same saved dishes, no new model calls; the full-agent column reflects the local recordings at that time). 8 of 8 restaurants completed. 24 model calls (the two Qloo-grounded arms used 32699 input and 4059 output tokens in total). The restaurants were fixed before the run: the ten in eval/restaurants.json minus the second pick in New York and in Los Angeles (Joe's Pizza, AC Barbeque), so each city counts once.

## What is measured

**Crowd lift**, the score Tastecraft shows on every playbook (src/lib/lift.ts): Qloo gives two sets of restaurants in the same city, the ~150 that this restaurant's audience has the highest affinity for (the crowd) and the city's ~150 most popular ones; only those with menu tags in Qloo count (second column below). Each dish name is split into the food terms local Qloo menu tags use; a term's lift is the share of crowd restaurants whose menu tags mention it divided by the share of city restaurants that do (add-one smoothed); dishes and plans use geometric means. ×1.00 = no different from the city; above 1 = this crowd's favourite places serve it more often.

Every arm is scored by the same function against the same two sets. All arms use the same model, the same effort and the app's own baseline prompt ("suggest three dishes to add and one event idea"); they differ only in the Qloo data appended to the prompt:

- **LLM only**: no Qloo data (the same prompt, model and effort as the app's quick no-Qloo answer).
- **+ Qloo affinity**: the audience's dish and cuisine tags and loved local places with Qloo affinity, i.e. what the earlier Taste-Fit score was built on.
- **+ Qloo crowd vs city**: the same, plus Tastecraft's crowd-vs-city contrast (the taste_gaps tool output).
- **Full agent**: the dishes of a real recorded Tastecraft playbook for that restaurant, rescored now (only where a local recording exists).

## Held-out check: does the crowd-vs-city gain survive on restaurants the model never saw?

The "+ Qloo crowd vs city" arm above is shown the same Qloo contrast its dishes are then scored on, so its result is partly circular. `npm run benchmark:holdout` (scripts/benchmark-holdout.ts, run on 2026-10-21) measures how much of the gain survives without that overlap. Each restaurant's crowd and city sets are split in two halves by a seeded hash of the Qloo entity id, so a restaurant falls in the same half of both sets. The held-out arm is given the contrast computed on half A only, and every arm's dishes are scored on half B only, with the same crowd-lift formula. The local places the guests love, which both Qloo arms see by name, are always kept in half A, so no scored restaurant was named to the model. 5 splits (seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) with one fresh sample per arm per split: 160 calls of the same model (`claude-opus-5-5`) at the same effort as above, 275981 input and 47920 output tokens (about US$2.06 at list prices). 8 of 8 restaurants completed.

**Reading.** On restaurants it was not shown, the contrast still moves the model's dishes towards what this crowd's favourite places serve, but by much less than the in-sample result above suggests. The held-out arm scores ×1.15 on half B against ×0.90 for the same model with no Qloo data: per restaurant, ×1.27 (95% interval ×1.07 to ×1.48). It beats LLM only in 6 of 8 restaurants (exact two-sided sign test p = 0.289, little power with 8) and in 29 of 40 plans. Against Qloo affinity data alone (×1.00) the ratio is ×1.15 (95% interval ×0.89 to ×1.53), which this sample cannot tell apart from no difference. The arm that saw all places scores ×1.72 on the same half B and beats the held-out arm in 8 of 8 restaurants, ×1.50 (95% interval ×1.34 to ×1.68): that gap is what seeing the scored places, and a contrast built on twice as many restaurants, adds, and it is why the in-sample result above overstates what the contrast is worth on restaurants the model has not seen.

Limits of this check: half-size sets make each term count noisier; one sample per arm per split; the intervals are over these 8 restaurants and say nothing about other cities or kinds of restaurant; and both halves come from the same city and the same Qloo audience signal, so this tests whether the contrast carries over to other restaurants this crowd loves, not whether guests would order the dishes.

## Why the old Taste-Fit score could not tell plans apart

For the top 10 dish tags of each audience, the mean Qloo affinity was 99.2 for the restaurant's audience and 99.6 for the city overall (no audience signal), averaged over 8 restaurants. Affinity puts what is popular everywhere near the top for almost any audience, so a mean affinity of cited facts reads ~96-100 for any plan.

## Limitations: what these results do not establish

- **Crowd lift is a proxy, not demand.** It says the crowd's favourite restaurants serve dishes like these more often than the city's most popular ones; it does not predict covers, revenue or whether guests will order the dish here.
- **The in-sample "crowd vs city" result is partly circular.** That arm sees the same Qloo contrast its dishes are scored on. Dish names are scored independently of what the model cites, so a high score still requires naming dishes the crowd's places actually serve, but the arm is expected to do well. The held-out check above removes the overlap; use its numbers for what the contrast is worth on restaurants the model has not seen.
- **The term matcher is a heuristic.** Dish names are matched to local menu tags by words (accents, case and simple plurals folded; preparation, season and format words ignored on their own). Creative names can match nothing (excluded from the score) or match a generic word.
- **Small samples.** 8 restaurants, one sample per arm, ~150 restaurants per set; per-term counts are often single digits. Differences per restaurant are noisy; look at the aggregate.
- **Coverage varies by city.** Qloo menu tags are richer in some cities than others.
- **The full-agent column** rescores the dish names of local recordings made during development; it is only shown where one exists.

Raw per-restaurant results (dish names, term counts) contain Qloo-derived data and stay in eval/benchmark.local.json, which is not committed.
